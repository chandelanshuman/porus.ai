## Slide 2 — The problem

Half the world can't hear you.

- India alone speaks in dozens of languages. A video made in one of them simply doesn't exist for the rest.
- Human dubbing is slow, expensive, and reserved for blockbusters. Everything else — training, lectures, announcements, creator content — never gets translated at all.
- Machine dubbing exists, but you can hear it. Lines run long and get squeezed. Voices drift mid-video. The music disappears. And when the machine isn't sure, it guesses.

**The result: organisations and creators either pay a fortune, ship something that sounds broken, or stay in one language.**

---

## Slide 3 — Why existing tools fall short

A translation is not a dub.

A dub has to land in the same moment as the original — against the picture, against the pause, against the breath the speaker took. The same sentence in Hindi needs more syllables than in English. Most tools solve this by squeezing the audio until it fits.

**That squeeze is what machine dubbing sounds like.** And most tools will always return *something* — which means some of what they return is wrong, and nobody catches it before it's published.

---

## Slide 4 — The solution

**Porus dubs the way a studio would — automatically.**

- **Lands on the beat.** Every line fits the moment it was spoken in. When a translation runs long, we rewrite it shorter instead of distorting the audio — inside a strict limit that keeps it sounding natural.
- **Your music survives.** Only the words change. The score, the atmosphere, the room — everything underneath the dialogue stays exactly as you made it.
- **Everyone keeps a voice.** Each speaker gets one consistent voice from the first line to the last, even when people talk over each other.
- **Nothing is guessed.** When a line can't be fitted cleanly, it isn't forced through — it's held for a human reviewer. Quality is a design decision, not luck.

---

## Slide 5 — How it feels to use

**Send it, review it, publish it.**

1. **Send a video.** No preparation, no scripts, no timecodes.
2. **We take it apart and put it back together.** Dialogue is lifted away from everything underneath it, replaced in the new language, and set back in place — in the same timing as the original.
3. **A person signs off.** Anything uncertain waits in a review queue. Fix one line and the rest of the video stays exactly as it was.
4. **You get everything back.** The dubbed video, the audio, subtitles and transcripts — plus your untouched original alongside it.

---

## Slide 6 — Built for trust

**Every line keeps its receipts.**

- Every change to every line is recorded — what the original said, what changed, why, and who approved it. Nothing is overwritten, ever.
- Reviewers edit one line at a time. A correction never risks the rest of the video.
- For organisations that can't send recordings outside — banks, hospitals, studios, government — Porus runs **entirely inside your own network**. Zero files leave. No uploads, no third-party cloud, no exception to argue for.

**This is the difference between a demo and something you can put your name on.**

# Appendix — Messaging pillars (for the website & collateral)

These are the four ideas everything above hangs on. Anything new you add to the site should ladder up to one of them.

1. **Timing** — "Not translated. Fitted." Every line lands where the original did.
2. **Sound** — "Only the words change." Music, atmosphere, and per-speaker voices survive.
3. **Trust** — "Nothing overwritten. Nothing guessed." Full audit trail, human review, self-hosted option.
4. **Honesty** — "We'd rather tell you the edges than let you find them." Publish limitations openly; it converts sceptics.

### Ideas to build on top of the current site

- **A hero demo clip** — a 15-second before/after with the audio toggle. The waveform visuals are great; real sound would close the deal.
- **A "send us something difficult" gallery** — anonymised examples of hard clips (overlapping speakers, loud music) with the result. Proof beats claims.
- **Testimonial or pilot logos** as they arrive — one line each, under the Trust pillar.
- **A pricing/contact page split by path** — organisations get "request a briefing," creators get a waitlist with an email capture, so leads self-sort.
- **An honest changelog** — "what got better this month" reinforces the honesty pillar and gives return visitors a reason to come back.
